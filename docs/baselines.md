# Baselines 1–3 — first end-to-end run

`qwen/qwen3.7-flash` pinned to `alibaba`, 76-tool corpus, 20 scenarios × 5 turns
(100 labeled turns), cold start, zero transport errors.

| arm | accuracy | hallucinated | cache hit | prompt tok/turn | hops | latency | $/turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| full catalog | **86.0%** | 0.0% | 94.2% | 10,088 | 0.00 | 2.0s | $0.000083 |
| RAG-over-tools (k=8) | 64.0% | 12.0% | 3.6% | 2,031 | 0.00 | 1.3s | $0.000080 |
| lazy discovery | 81.0% | 0.0% | 16.7% | 3,677 | 0.84 | 2.0s | $0.000105 |

## The result the project exists to exploit

Relative to the full catalog:

| arm | prompt tokens | cost |
|---|---|---|
| RAG-over-tools | **20.1%** | **96.4%** |
| lazy discovery | **36.4%** | **126.5%** |

**A 5× spread in prompt tokens produces at most a 26% spread in cost.** Cutting
tokens without preserving the prefix does not cut spend: RAG-over-tools drops 80%
of the tokens and saves 4%, because rebuilding the prefix each turn re-bills every
remaining token at the uncached rate (3.6% hit vs 94.2%). Lazy discovery keeps the
prefix tiny but pays 0.84 extra round trips per turn and ends up *more* expensive
than sending everything.

HotSet's target is therefore not "fewer tokens" but "fewer tokens that are also
*stable*" — match 86.0% accuracy at a real fraction of $0.000083.

## Accuracy ceilings

BM25 top-k recall on this suite bounds what RAG-over-tools can possibly reach:

| catalog | top-1 | top-5 | top-8 | top-20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 76 | 41.0% | 59.0% | 66.0% | 74.0% |
| 150 | 38.0% | 56.0% | 58.0% | 68.0% |
| 300 | 32.0% | 48.0% | 52.0% | 59.0% |
| 500 | 26.0% | 39.0% | 48.0% | 57.0% |

At k=8 the tool is simply absent from the prompt on 34% of turns at 76 tools and
52% at 500, which is where its 12% hallucination rate comes from. HotSet has no
such ceiling: layer A keeps every name visible for ~39 tokens, so a tool outside
the hot set is still nameable and tail-loadable. Retrieval failure costs HotSet a
tail-load; it costs RAG-over-tools the task.

## Two fairness corrections made before recording these numbers

Both were our bugs, not properties of the baselines.

1. **Lazy discovery was crippled by the shared preamble** telling every arm to
   "call a tool by its exact name" — advice that invites guessing when the model
   has been shown no names. Removing it when no names are present, and dropping the
   dispatcher (whose description claims to be *the* way to call a tool, competing
   with `search_tools`), moved the arm from 15.0% / 69% hallucination to
   81.0% / 0%. A baseline this weak would have made the comparison worthless.
2. **Cross-run cache contamination.** Re-running an arm inside the provider's cache
   TTL inherits a warm prefix it never paid for. The same RAG arm measured 2.8% hit
   / $0.000081 cold and 89.7% / $0.000024 warm — a 3.4× cost swing larger than any
   effect under test. Every run now injects one nonce at the head of the preamble,
   shared by all arms and sessions in that run.

## Task suite

20 scenarios × 5 turns, generated by Claude Sonnet 5 and frozen to `data/tasks.json`.
Queries are natural user goals forbidden from reusing tool names or description
wording — paraphrasing descriptions would hand the lexical baselines a free win.
Covers 34 distinct tools across 7 servers; git, playwright, filesystem and memory
dominate, which matches real agent usage but leaves the long tail untested.
